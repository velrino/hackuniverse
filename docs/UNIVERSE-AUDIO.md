# Universe audio

Open Skies supplies the background music. Interface and camera effects are imported from the supplied ARK Starmap sound collection; they are third-party recordings, not HackUniverse-authored or newly generated effects. [Asset credits](ASSET-CREDITS.md) records their source. [generation.json](../assets/audio/generation.json) and [manifest.json](../assets/audio/manifest.json) distinguish music generation, imported recordings, processing and file checksums.

## Music and effect assets

Open Skies retains its original ElevenLabs-generated recording, processed FLAC and runtime MP3 unchanged. Its prompt requests a sparse, hopeful synthetic space ambience without vocals, beats or a prominent melody. The 96-second source uses a six-second equal-power wrap crossfade to produce a 90-second loop. The runtime is stereo 44.1 kHz/96 kbps, 1,080,961 bytes, measured at -22.44 LUFS and -10.55 dBTP. Numerical boundary checks do not establish that the seam is perceptually inaudible.

The imported sound catalog contains 45 WAV recordings recursively, excluding AmbientMusic and Unity metadata. Canonical WAVs under [imported/starmap](../assets/audio/imported/starmap) retain identical decoded PCM with metadata removed. The corresponding MP3 catalog under `web/public/audio/catalog/starmap` is available lazily; the audio module does not fetch that entire catalog. Digitally silent Unused recordings remain documented catalog entries rather than being assigned to invented product actions.

Only these nine aliases are preloaded for real interactions:

| Runtime alias under `/audio/sfx/` | Interaction |
| --- | --- |
| `menu-open.mp3` | Open the universe menu |
| `menu-close.mp3` | Close the universe menu |
| `ui-select.mp3` | Activate an ordinary enabled interface control |
| `camera-orbit.mp3` | Rotate the camera with a real single-pointer drag |
| `zoom-in.mp3`, `zoom-out.mp3` | Approach or move away manually or during explicit tour travel |
| `toggle-on.mp3`, `toggle-off.mp3` | Commit an enabled switch or toggle change |
| `destination-hover.mp3` | Physically move the pointer onto a new destination |

Camera rotation uses the complete approximately 2.333-second CAMRotate recording, and both zoom aliases use the complete approximately 5.333-second CAMZoom recording. These quiet continuous source recordings are normalized with constant gain, not treated as silent padding. The playback lease below ends their browser voice with manual manipulation or actual guided-tour travel. The processed MP3 durations include frame padding; the manifest records the measured values and levels. The active aliases total 159,971 bytes. Other SystemAmbient and Unused recordings have no runtime action or eager loading assignment.

Earlier generated and user-supplied effect candidates remain archived under `assets/audio/drafts`, including the complete pre-Starmap set. They are no longer active browser effects. None of these replacements changes Open Skies or imports the excluded AmbientMusic recording.

## Playback and preferences

The global [audio module](../web/src/modules/audio/index.ts) exports `AudioProvider`, `AudioControls`, `useAudio` and public types. The application mounts the provider once. Universe adapters supply optional semantic callbacks; the renderer owns no audio provider or asset URL.

The provider memoizes its controller, and effect cleanup disposes the previous instance before mounting a replacement. Fast Refresh recalculates that memo, so updated controller code takes effect without remounting the surrounding application or retaining the old controller's closures. Use a fresh document when validating a deployment; an already open page can still be running its previously loaded JavaScript.

Settings places Language first and Sound second. Sound contains independent Music and Sound effects switches and volumes. With no valid saved preference, both switches default to On, at 35% music and 50% effects volume. Existing version-1 preferences preserve their exact On/Off choices and volumes, including Off after refresh. The provider reads `hackuniverse.audio.v1` before allocating resources, using the shared lazy storage transport. An unreadable preference leaves both channels Off with an honest warning instead of guessing a possibly saved opt-out. These preferences are separate from Local universe exports.

Enabled channels attempt playback only while the document is visible. Browser autoplay policy remains authoritative. A waiting or blocked channel retries on the first trusted activation-producing interaction: mouse press, touch/pen release or an ordinary non-repeating key press. Both gesture-sensitive calls start synchronously. Where available, the browser's user-activation state is checked as well. Audio controls, disabled/inert targets, modifier-only keys and synthetic events do not unlock playback; clicking Off cannot first start a blocked channel. Pending gesture attempts and a 750 ms gate prevent retry storms. No extra Resume button is required per visit. On a first visit that needs browser activation, a single Enter universe action starts the three-second welcome and both enabled audio channels. Once effects are ready, the welcome plays `menu-open` once while it remains visible; a late load never queues that cue after the welcome has closed. The seen flag is saved before the welcome countdown opens. Saved silence and zero-volume settings do not require an audio entry action. An unseen tour can offer its own Start tour action when the welcome was already seen and the new document still needs activation; an entry gesture already received by the document avoids a second prompt during slow audio loading. Actual load/unavailable errors use one Retry action. See the browser [activation contract](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/Defenses/User_activation) and [autoplay guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay).

Music is streamed through a looping media element and Web Audio gain; the full track is not decoded into a memory buffer. Each active effect is fetched and decoded with a 512 KiB, eight-second and two-channel cap. Music play, effect loading and context activation each have a 15-second deadline. A suspended activation timeout is waiting for interaction; an already running context with stalled media remains a load error.

## Interaction feedback

Ordinary UI effects retain a 100 ms global cooldown, 180 ms per-cue cooldown and a three-voice budget. Toggle feedback reads committed checked/pressed semantics, including controls that close their dialog. Selected destination labels retain selection semantics rather than being mistaken for toggles. Sound settings remain silent controls, so muting does not itself emit an effect.

Camera feedback follows real manual manipulation. Trusted wheel and pointer packets older than 180 ms cannot renew audio, so an input backlog does not replay sounds after a busy rendering task. Release and cancellation always remain effective, regardless of packet age. Each accepted wheel, pinch or zoom-control movement renews a 180 ms audio lease; orbit uses 120 ms. A continuing same-direction gesture keeps its current voice playing without periodically restarting the recording. A direction change uses a short eight-millisecond handoff. Zoom bypasses ordinary UI cooldowns so a recent click cannot hide manipulation feedback. Selecting a destination, automatic idle orbit and ordinary camera tweens never originate or renew a camera cue. The explicit guided tour has separate provider-neutral zoom callbacks: only measured travel in its own camera tween or hierarchy transition renews zoom feedback. Its arrival, holds, cancellation, blocked controls, hidden state and teardown end that feedback. The Earth hold can rotate visually without emitting rotation effects. Music continues through travel and holds according to the saved channel settings. A still-live manual zoom can retain its existing voice across its own adjacent hierarchy transition; that setup never extends the audio deadline.

Manual orbit requires actual azimuth/polar travel from a trusted single-pointer drag and renews its own movement lease. Pointer-down without movement, a stationary held pointer, two-finger pinch, zoom-only input, automatic motion and flight emit no orbit cue. Small flicks are checked after input dispatch, using the camera change from that physical pointer event. Reduced motion retains deliberate user input and its feedback.

Pointer/pinch release or cancellation explicitly ends the associated camera gesture through the optional provider-neutral `onCameraGestureEnd` callback. An audible voice fades out over about 20 ms; a handoff scheduled to start later is cancelled immediately. Idle zoom expires after 180 ms without renewal, and idle orbit after 120 ms, followed by the same bounded fade.

Each renewal schedules both the gain release and `AudioBufferSourceNode.stop()` against `AudioContext.currentTime`, updating the same live source's future deadline. This [audio-clock stop](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioScheduledSourceNode/stop) remains scheduled while the JavaScript/rendering thread is busy; the JavaScript lease timer also clears domain ownership. An expired or explicitly stopped source rejects renewal and cannot resume. A naturally completed or expired clip can start again only on a new real movement sample; there is no timer that periodically restarts or queues a camera recording. State identity and revision checks keep stale lease callbacks from stopping a newer gesture.

Zoom and orbit each own one movement layer and coexist within the three-voice budget. At capacity, movement replaces an ordinary UI voice while preserving the other movement layer. Gesture endings leave menu effects and music alone. Hover feedback only follows real mouse/pen motion onto a new label or core. A planet moving beneath a stationary pointer can update its visual highlight without emitting audio. Dragging, tours and touch input suppress hover intent.

Hiding the page pauses music, aborts pending effects, suspends contexts and clears active, fading and scheduled voices. Returning may resume enabled channels that were playing or loading, subject to browser permission. A document mounted hidden waits until visible. Off, zero volume, hidden state and disposal never queue old gestures for later playback. Failures leave the visual explorer usable.

## Validation boundaries

The manifest owns exact durations, source identity, processing levels and hashes. Automated checks cover opt-out restoration, activation gating, simultaneous channel startup, cancellation, stale callbacks, lease renewal/expiry, bounded voices, movement feedback and disposal. They include fourteen zoom samples spaced 140 ms apart, native deadlines with delayed JavaScript callbacks, actual audio-thread PCM measurements during a blocked rendering task, and a new sample after audio-clock expiry. [Issue #42](https://github.com/velrino/hackuniverse/issues/42) tracks gesture-scoped playback, and [issue #43](https://github.com/velrino/hackuniverse/issues/43) tracks zoom continuity and bounded release under rendering load. Listen to gesture release, idle expiry and the music loop in fresh desktop/mobile browser documents; browser emulation and numeric checks do not establish physical-device playback quality or performance. First-visit browser journeys additionally verify entry activation, one welcome cue, tour music/zoom, Skip cancellation, saved silence and pending-prompt removal when reduced motion is enabled. [Issue #44](https://github.com/velrino/hackuniverse/issues/44) tracks this tour and welcome integration. Publication state is tracked separately from technical provenance.
