# Universe audio

The audio work is a local preview awaiting user listening validation. Open Skies and three menu/selection effects use generated ElevenLabs recordings; both zoom directions share a user-supplied cinematic clip. Runtime files retain the same modular playback API. Publication and physical-device listening approval remain separate from provenance and technical checks.

## Direction and provenance

Open Skies is a minimal, hopeful deep-space ambience: a sparse warm synthetic drone, uplifting major-key harmony, luminous atmospheric textures and occasional delicate crystal sparkles. The prompt requests an instrumental 96-second source and uses a felt tempo around 65 BPM as a pacing cue while explicitly excluding an audible pulse, beat, rhythmic patterns, arpeggios, drums and percussion. There are no requested vocals or choir, and dark, sad, ominous or mysterious moods are excluded. The atmosphere stays bright, spacious and consistent, with no introduction, ending, final cadence or silence. These are generation instructions, not a measurement of the resulting recording or proof that the loop is inaudible.

The three generated menu/selection effects use a restrained aerospace-instrumentation brief: close, dry tactile microclicks and small electrical transients. Their prompts request precise, realistic, calm feedback without pitched notes, bloops, bells or cartoon character. Source requests use 0.5 seconds; runtime processing trims/fades these three cues to 0.35 seconds and filters them from 110 Hz to 6.5 kHz.

Zoom uses the supplied `Futuristic_cinematic_#3-1793355528036.mp3`, preserved byte-for-byte as [user-zoom-cinematic.mp3](../assets/audio/source/user-zoom-cinematic.mp3). The complete approximately 0.6065-second recording plays identically for zoom-in and zoom-out, with no trim, reversal or pitch change. Its exact supplied camera-assembly/zoom prompt and filename are recorded under `imports` in [generation.json](../assets/audio/generation.json); this import made no ElevenLabs API request. The user-supplied recording and prompt are provenance, not evidence of a newly generated output. Ship engines and travel cutscenes are not implemented by this set.

[generation.json](../assets/audio/generation.json) separates generated requests from user-provided imports, retaining model IDs, prompts, durations, paths and status. Canonical recordings live under [assets/audio/source](../assets/audio/source). The separate [processed manifest](../assets/audio/manifest.json) records source/derivative durations, sizes, loudness, true peaks and SHA-256 hashes. Generation credentials and account details stay outside source and public assets. The browser plays local files and does not call ElevenLabs or require a generation key.

The [processed FLAC master](../assets/audio/masters/open-skies-loop.flac) preserves this edit, but its input is a generated MP3; it is not an original lossless generation source. Level adjustment uses constant gain rather than time-varying normalization across the seam. The runtime stereo MP3 is 44.1 kHz/96 kbps and 1,080,961 bytes, with measured integrated loudness of -22.44 LUFS and true peak of -10.55 dBTP. This music file is unchanged by the effects replacement.

Each menu/selection effect remains a 0.35-second mono 44.1 kHz/64 kbps MP3 of 3,386 bytes, with short endpoint fades and a 110 Hz–6.5 kHz passband. Their integrated LUFS is left null; the -25 dBFS mean-level and -9 dBFS pre-MP3 headroom targets remain separate from measured encoded results. These three files and Open Skies are unchanged by the zoom replacement.

Both zoom runtime files contain the same complete clip, attenuated by 5 dB with a 4 ms start fade and 8 ms end fade, encoded as stereo 44.1 kHz/96 kbps MP3s of 8,194 bytes each. Full sequential decode measures approximately 0.6065 seconds; MP3 container timing and encoder padding should not be mistaken for a deliberate content trim. The five effect files total 26,546 bytes, and all six runtime files total 1,107,507 bytes. The manifest records source and processed levels separately.

## Playback contract

Open the universe menu's Settings tab and use Sound for independent music/effects controls and volumes. Both start disabled; default volumes are 35% for music and 50% for effects. Enabling either requires an explicit user gesture. Restoring an enabled preference does not grant autoplay permission or start a new document's playback automatically; press Resume for that visit. The versioned `hackuniverse.audio.v1` preference belongs to the current browser origin and remains separate from the Local universe export.

The [audio module's public entry](../web/src/modules/audio/index.ts) exports `AudioProvider`, `AudioControls`, `useAudio` and its public types. The application mounts the provider globally; optional universe audio adapters supply semantic menu/zoom cues and the Settings controls. The domain owns playback intent through provider-neutral music/effects ports. [Browser adapters](../web/src/modules/audio/adapters/browser-audio.ts) own media loading, decoding and resources: music uses a looping media element with no initial preload, routed through a Web Audio gain for volume control without decoding the full track into memory; effects are fetched/decoded only after enabling. No audio asset requests are needed for the initial disabled state.

UI selection comes from enabled click targets, including keyboard activation, with audio controls and specialized menu/zoom triggers excluded from duplicate generic cues. The soundtrack loops without owning the camera, tour, workspace or flight controller. Effects must not be emitted for every animation frame: the controller bounds playback to three simultaneous voices, with a 100 ms global cooldown and 180 ms per-cue cooldown. Zoom additionally owns one shared playback slot: new zoom requests, including a direction reversal, are ignored until the current clip finishes instead of stacking, restarting or cutting it. Ordinary UI cues can continue within the normal voice budget.

Hiding the page pauses music, suspends effects and clears active voices. Returning may resume audio that was already playing, subject to browser permission. Music playback, effects loading and AudioContext resume each have a 15-second deadline. Off/loading/error states remain distinct, and retry is a user action. Missing media, rejected playback or unavailable browser audio must leave the visual explorer usable. Disabling a channel stops its playback; disposal cancels loading and releases resources.

## Validation and release state

The processed manifest marks the audio as `local-preview-awaiting-user-listening-validation`, with `listeningApproval` and `physicalDeviceValidation` unset. Technical durations, formats, levels and derivative checksums are recorded there. Its numerical loop-boundary checks use a full sequential decode, retaining MP3 reservoir/encoder-delay behavior; they remain technical checks rather than listening approval. Listen to repeated loop boundaries and effect tails, then check music/effect toggles, independent volumes, gesture gating, refresh, blocked playback, visibility changes and cleanup in desktop/mobile browsers. Automated decoding or a numeric boundary check cannot establish that a seam is perceptually inaudible. Browser emulation is not physical-device listening, battery or performance validation.

The project has not recorded plan-specific licensing approval for the generated recordings or publication rights for the supplied zoom recording. Provenance and a working preview do not establish publication clearance. Consult the applicable [Eleven Music terms](https://elevenlabs.io/music-terms) and [ElevenCreative terms](https://elevenlabs.io/creative-terms) against the generating account and intended distribution, and retain the supplied clip's separate rights record. Official API references describe [music composition](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/music/compose) and [sound-effect generation](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-sound-effects/convert); they do not replace those provenance/rights records.

Music, interface sound, optional flight audio and travel presentation remain separate roadmap scopes. This preview adds no engine loop, spatial flight audio, travel stinger or Space Jump cutscene. [Asset credits](ASSET-CREDITS.md) distinguish these generated recordings from the visual and mood references.
