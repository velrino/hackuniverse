# Immersive universe demo

The home route is a full-viewport 3D demo with **738 planets and 871 total entities** across 107 systems, 20 stellar clouds and five galaxies, from [the editable canonical catalog](../web/src/modules/universe/data/demo-universe.json). Earth remains the initial target. The Milky Way now contains eight clouds, 67 systems and 338 planets; seven clouds are explicitly fictional demo sectors. Solar Neighborhood remains unchanged at eleven systems and 58 planets. Membership and spacing are illustrative, not measured distances or a confirmed exoplanet inventory. All bundled entities explicitly carry optional `type: "demo"`; ordinary objects omit that classification, independently of catalog mode. A hide-demo filter is not implemented. [The data contract](UNIVERSE-DATA.md) owns the fixture breakdown and boundaries. No account content, jobs or provider requests are created.

## Interaction contract

- The original 400 demo planets represent fictional websites; the eight Solar System objects use familiar names and URLless references. The canonical contract also supports other URLless objects, such as documents or tickets. Systems and stellar clouds are visual groupings under galaxies and a universe. The rendered star is a system anchor, not another catalog record. These view groupings do not silently change the planned persistent account/catalog model.
- Selecting a planet moves the camera smoothly within its existing system over 900 ms. The star, sibling planets, orbits, geometry identities and orbital state remain intact. Clicking a group also animates the approach before entering its detail; interacting with the camera cancels the pending approach. Returning to the system restores the saved overview rather than rebuilding a one-object scene.
- Every system's central star is selectable by clicking its mesh or activating its keyboard-accessible label. Selection fits the intact system and opens `StarDetails`; selecting it again reframes the camera. The Sun uses optional system-star metadata and never adds a planet to counts. Known physical facts and a validated source appear in the panel; missing facts are omitted. Flight ignores central-star selection, and autopilot targets planets only.
- Selected planets enable a gentle automatic camera orbit by default. Settings can disable it without changing selection or language. Manual gestures briefly suspend the orbit; menus, flight, hierarchy/camera transitions and reduced motion suppress it. Its elapsed-time update keeps speed independent of the render budget.
- The unobtrusive hamburger opens one bounded modal with Go to, Settings and About tabs. Go to searches every catalog planet regardless of camera scale; 24 results render per page, with accessible previous/next controls. Settings groups workspace, sound, view, flight and language controls; About provides project information. English and Português (Brasil) appear directly in Settings with flags and the current selection, without a second modal. Language changes preserve the current scene, menu tab, page and query. Escape opens the menu and pauses active flight; there is no standalone pause toolbar.
- The wider-view action steps through system, cloud, galaxy and universe. It disables the ship before showing a wider map. Physical camera zoom remains available through wheel/pinch; it is distinct from changing the logical grouping.
- The Space Jump presentation is temporarily disabled. Choosing another system navigates without a timed delay or full-screen travel dialog; normal camera focus and hierarchy morphs remain active. The travel hook and cutscene source are retained for future use but are not mounted by the explorer, so they schedule no timer and do not pause navigation.
- Third-person flight is optional and confined to the current system. Desktop input uses WASD, Q/E and Shift; touch controls appear on devices with coarse pointers. Opening the menu, hiding the page or losing focus clears held inputs. Leaving flight restores the map camera. Wider views never render the ship.

Planet and star panel names/descriptions use shared `TypewriterText`, with a 25 ms nominal delay and 3,500 ms maximum duration. Only writing is animated; facts, demo markers and actions appear immediately. A hidden full-text reserve prevents card reflow, screen readers receive one complete copy, and reduced motion shows the full text immediately. The bounded cards keep scrolling available for long descriptions. See [the public API example](../web/README.md#typewriter-text).

## Guided demo tour

`DemoUniverseHost` supplies an optional tour definition outside the canonical catalog schema. The automatic run waits for both scene readiness and the global welcome loader to close. It focuses Earth over 900 ms, then holds the arrived camera for three seconds with a gentle orbit when motion is enabled. Arrival acknowledgements start the hold; loading and camera approach do not consume it. The outer steps then widen through Solar System, Solar Neighborhood, Milky Way and universe at a constant perceptual zoom rate of `0.9` natural-log distance units per second. Durations derive from each distance ratio, with 350 ms initial acceleration and 450 ms final deceleration. Intermediate arrivals hand off immediately with no dwell, maintaining the apparent rate across hierarchy scales. Ordinary navigation easing remains unchanged.

While the tour is active, the normal explorer HUD and scene labels are hidden. Only a bottom-centered panel remains, showing `Tour · destination` and Skip tour. Its surface background is 50% transparent while text and the button remain opaque; safe-area spacing and a 44-pixel action target keep it usable on mobile. The normal HUD and labels return when the tour ends or is cancelled.

Manual replay starts from the current visible camera pose. The pure `buildManualUniverseTour` helper finds the current effective group (a selected planet uses its parent system), walks adjacent groups through the lowest common ancestor toward the configured first target, then focuses that target. For the bundled demo this reaches Earth, holds for one second after camera arrival, and follows the unchanged outward steps. Approach steps use `motion: "approach"` and ordinary camera/hierarchy easing, preserving the live pose through handoffs. `useUniverseTour.start(preparedSteps?)` snapshots this route while awaiting scene, intro, menu and flight readiness. The automatic first-visit route retains its original three-second Earth hold.

The once flag is `hackuniverse.demo-tour.v1.seen`, persisted before the first camera action. It is separate from the global intro's flag. Settings contains a manual replay action. Camera input, navigation, menu opening or hiding the page cancels the run; pending timers and stale arrival callbacks cannot advance a cancelled tour. Reduced motion skips automatic playback while allowing manual replay without camera tweening. This camera presentation does not enable Space Jump or measure loading progress.

## Camera links

Settings → Share this view contains Copy view link. It reads the current camera at the time of the click and copies the destination and pose; when clipboard access fails, a selectable View link field provides a manual fallback. The URL contract is:

```text
?lang=en&source=demo&target=planet-earth&camera=px,py,pz,tx,ty,tz
```

`target` is a canonical entity ID. `camera` contains the eye position followed by the look-at target, in illustrative scene display units, rounded to six decimal places. Planet coordinates are relative to that planet's current center, so orbital phase does not invalidate its framing; other poses are local to their hierarchy view. Restoring a link still applies that level's zoom limits and mobile framing. A link does not reproduce flight, tour progress or a physical astronomical position.

The optional `source` is `demo` or `local` when the application host supplies that identity. An omitted source keeps the current source. An explicit source must match the resolved catalog, and the target must exist there. Local links contain no workspace payload and upload nothing: the receiving browser needs the same objects and IDs, for example through [manual JSON transfer](LOCAL-WORKSPACE.md#json-import-and-export). Unavailable destinations or sources produce a notice and leave the default view available.

The codec requires one complete `target`/`camera` pair and rejects repeated parameters, malformed numbers and unsupported sources. The target and camera fields are each limited to 256 characters; target IDs cannot contain control characters. Each of the six coordinates must be finite with an absolute value at most `10,000,000`, and eye-to-target separation must be between `0.001` and `20,000,000` display units. Invalid links produce a notice rather than becoming scene commands.

The host restores a valid pose once its destination and selection are ready, after any active tour or loading/flight camera ownership ends. Restoration cancels competing camera tweens, and direct entry suppresses the automatic demo tour; manual replay remains available. Back/forward navigation reads the new URL and cancels pending writes. Locale-only changes and catalog edits do not reapply an acknowledged pose.

Manual camera intent and navigation schedule a report after 350 ms, waiting for active gestures and camera transitions to finish; URL writes are then coalesced for 200 ms. Same-document `replaceState` preserves the pathname, language, unrelated query parameters and hash without adding a history entry or remounting the application. Auto-orbit and tours do not serialize the camera per frame; finishing or cancelling a tour publishes its final view once. Camera-only URL changes are excluded from accepted pageviews by [the telemetry policy](OBSERVABILITY.md#events-and-application-scope).

The optional location adapter supplies URL, clipboard and navigation integration outside the renderer. The scene receives provider-neutral restore/read/report ports; it does not own browser routing, source selection or workspace transfer.

## Scene detail and scale

Camera distance drives semantic navigation as well as optical zoom. Recent wheel/pinch input can move from a planet back to its intact system, then to its cloud, galaxy and universe. Enter/exit thresholds and a cooldown prevent bouncing between levels; camera tweens and piloting do not trigger these transitions. A validated user crossing survives a short cooldown instead of requiring another wheel impulse, and is consumed once or cancelled on reversal, navigation, menu or flight. Labels relay wheel input to the canvas. Zooming near the projected bounds of a group under the cursor or pinch midpoint can enter its detail. A normal system view does not automatically select a random planet when approaching its central star.

The universe overview sets the outer zoom limit, preventing indefinite pullback beyond its fitted scale. Mouse or pen hover over any destination or its label shows a pointer cursor and activates that destination's native material highlight. Existing cores, particles, mini planets, globes and rings brighten; no destination receives an added circular aura, Sprite or overlay. Orbit lines and the outer background remain unchanged. Textured planet globes use a restrained emissive cue without recoloring their base surface. Leaving or cancelling hover restores the captured material values. Hover does not select or navigate; dragging, menus, flight, tours and camera/view transitions suppress it and clear transient state.

Planet selection uses one reusable cyan geodesic shell close to the globe, with a tight Fresnel atmospheric rim and at most three draws. The rim sits at `1.023 ×` the physical radius; the detail-two icosahedral wire sits at `1.045 ×`, with maximum opacity `0.18`, and eight subtle nodes sit at `1.046 ×`. The shell follows the actual planet transform without replacing its texture, recoloring its palette or adding a pulse. Projected size controls detail: wire is hidden below 18 pixels and nodes below 80 pixels. The shell hides when the camera reaches `1.05 ×` the planet radius. Reduced motion introduces no independent selection animation. Stars and cluster selections retain their existing thin ring treatment.

Galaxy, cloud and system picking targets their central region with a proxy radius of `0.24 × marker extent`; decorative outer particles are not the pick surface. Physical planet mesh targets remain unchanged. Every destination name anchors to its projected center, with text normally 28 screen pixels above it. The 44-pixel label targets, collision handling and reserved HUD space remain active; labels can move or hide when space is unavailable. Labels and Go to search keep small central targets reachable on mobile. These interaction defaults are shared with [the visual reference guide](DESIGN-SYSTEM.md#3d-destination-feedback).

Nested map detail comes from the actual catalog descendants. Wider cloud views represent systems as stars with small orbital detail; galaxies contain their system/cloud groups, and the universe contains galaxies. Shared, normalized layout scales keep parents visibly larger and farther apart than their contents. This is a synthetic navigable hierarchy, not a measured astronomical dataset, real parsec/AU map or gravitational simulation.

Detailed system previews require a projected extent of at least 28 screen pixels. The scene shows at most four detailed previews below 640 pixels wide, eight otherwise, choosing the largest candidates; remaining systems use lightweight star proxies. Hidden mini planets skip per-frame position updates and recover from the shared absolute orbital phase when detailed previews return. The catalog and clickable destinations remain complete at every detail level.

Crossing a hierarchy boundary preserves the camera angle and the previous world's screen projection. Shared preview layouts and orbital phases align the same descendants at both scales; a 720 ms overlap expands or collapses the detail while the outgoing context fades. Only one outgoing world is retained, then disposed. Outgoing labels and hit targets are removed, incoming labels wait for the handoff, and reduced motion replaces the view immediately. Continued gestures retain camera control while the overlap finishes; menu opening and subsequent navigation safely finalize it.

Surrounding stellar context persists across hierarchy levels. The optional scene `anchor` and root-to-parent `ancestors` retain the nearest galaxy's 640-particle spiral through galaxy, cloud and system detail. Its context core is visible only in galaxy detail; nearby cloud/system views retain particles and neighboring stars without ancestor haze. Native cloud haze appears on distant cloud markers within the galaxy view. Exact parent-to-child coordinate transforms use the original full sibling arrays, positions and preview scales, preserving the outlines through forward and reverse transitions. Shared visual factories and the same elapsed clock keep native previews and context aligned. Planet focus already preserves its intact system and this surrounding context.

Other ancestor siblings appear in one merged semantic-marker draw, capped at twelve below 640 pixels scene width and twenty-four otherwise. The budget reserves up to two/four galaxy markers, four/eight cloud markers and six/twelve system markers on mobile/desktop; unused slots go to the nearest candidates in current coordinates. The active branch is excluded, and no catalog objects are invented. System markers suggest warm stars and orbits, clouds use constellation-like dots, and galaxies use elliptical spiral glyphs. At tiny projected sizes they simplify to points; glyphs are capped at twenty CSS pixels. At most two passive names appear on mobile and four on desktop, fading with projected extent and adding no hit targets.

Galaxy dust, its optional core and the sibling marker batch total at most three draws per context root. No additional haze, catalog records or navigation anchors are created. The context refreshes on view or mobile-breakpoint changes, shares existing resources, and its geometry/materials follow the view group's disposal lifecycle. A finite enclosing radius keeps it within the camera's far range. Paused and reduced-motion scenes keep the existing motion policy, and normal hierarchy transitions retain only one outgoing root before disposal.

The star uses an original warm yellow/orange procedural surface and corona. A low-resolution procedural nebula panorama supplies the distant backdrop. Finite star layers move in perspective during dolly/pan; pulling back progressively reveals brighter, denser stars. Their transforms follow hierarchy handoffs, and direct-entry scales use the same shared preview ratios. One merged star draw contains 3,600 decorative points with no per-frame geometry rebuild. The separate foreground nebula wisps were removed to keep the map clear. These stars are background decoration, separate from catalog records and picking. UI accents and orbit lines remain cyan.

Each galaxy uses one bounded spiral buffer of 640 seeded multicolor particles, independent of its catalog descendant count. A shared 32×32 neutral white-alpha mask keeps the particles circular without tinting their hues. Additive blending makes the core and particles luminous by default; hover adds a stronger neutral brightness gain without replacing their colors. Switching between baseline and hover reuses the current geometry without additional draw calls, with particles capped at five CSS pixels even in close views. The existing DPR and four-mobile/eight-desktop detailed-preview budgets remain unchanged. Decorative spiral dust rotates slowly; catalog cloud haze drifts on the same motion clock as planetary orbits. These effects pause with the menu and reduced motion without moving the catalog's navigation anchors.

The original 400 fictional website planets keep their existing seeds and 100-color palette. The Solar System keeps its established profiles, with reserved visual-seed overrides for neutral gray Mercury and faint Neptune rings; the renderer never checks catalog IDs or suppliers. Neptune’s ring treatment is an illustrative visual choice informed by [NASA’s ring observations](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/new-webb-image-captures-clearest-view-of-neptunes-rings-in-decades/), not a measured scale model. Neighboring bodies and the added fictional sectors use the same procedural profile system. The profile supplies the globe, preview and atlas swatch; refresh, locale and camera changes never reroll it. Eight surface families preserve the selected hue while varying terrain, bands, craters, ice and storms. Radius, axial tilt, material and optional rings also derive from the seed. Sizes, spacing, surfaces and motion remain illustrative. Planet colors are independent of the cyan interface palette.

The first five orbital radii remain stable; additional orbits keep solid-body clearance for the largest profile. Each system batches fine cyan orbit guides into two draws: 160-segment continuous paths and restrained short accents. Opacity follows projected size, ranging from `0.075` to `0.2` for paths and `0.04` to `0.14` for accents. At most 64 orbit radii are drawn; larger systems retain the first eight and sample the rest through the outermost orbit. This bounds visual guides without removing planets. All 20 mock planets can receive labels, with collision handling controlling their visibility and selection priority.

Physical planetary rings retain their seeded profile, radius, tilt and palette. An analytic radial shader adds concentric bands and gaps with derivative-aware minification, preserving hover and transition fades. It allocates no image texture and adds no per-frame geometry work. Shared factories keep previews and full-size planets consistent without changing camera, scale or orbital phase.

Planetary orbital translation runs at 1.5 times its previous rate: the shared normalized speed coefficient is `0.09`, previously `0.06`. Full systems and their previews use the same orbital law and phase, preserving alignment during handoffs. Render budgets remain unchanged; these display values do not claim physical orbital periods or measured device performance.

The starter ship is rendered at one quarter of its first prototype size: a maximum span of `.375` local units. Cruise/boost targets are `2`/`5` local units per second, with lighter vertical/turn rates. Original lightweight engine plumes respond to thrust/boost and clean up with the flight session. These are deliberate arcade tuning values, not physical orbital units.

During flight, selecting a planet keeps the pilot active and exposes a localized contextual guidance action. Guidance follows the moving target with a safe standoff and bounded obstacle avoidance; manual movement cancels it. Pausing preserves a destination without accumulating movement, and leaving the system or losing the target cancels the approach. It never starts imports or creates catalog content.

## Module boundaries

`UniverseHost` receives a validated canonical catalog and supplies localization, branding, navigation links and a dynamically imported renderer. `DemoUniverseHost` chooses the default dataset for the home route; the generic host does not import it. Core adapters are consumer-owned, and feature composition uses the navigation hook and pure UI. Supplier normalization stays outside the domain and renderer; the default JSON is already canonical. See [the data guide](UNIVERSE-DATA.md) for the parser, public import boundary and optional Firecrawl adapter.

The renderer owns scene/camera/input lifecycles and receives a provider-free scene projection. Detail UI uses canonical name/description and optional reference metadata, including objects without URLs. Demo mode controls illustrative labels and suppresses outbound reference actions; it is not inferred from a supplier name. No view starts imports or creates catalog data.

Three.js and OrbitControls load only for the home renderer. GLTFLoader and the starter spacecraft load only after an explicit flight request. The ship helper owns the chase camera while loading or flying; the map controller suspends its own camera writers until that ownership ends. Stale model loads are aborted or disposed, and all GPU resources, observers, input listeners and animation frames are cleaned up on disposal.

## Performance and accessibility

The scene uses shared low-segment geometry, original 128/256-pixel procedural planet textures, a point starfield, capped device pixel ratio and no remote planet images, shadow maps or expensive bloom stack. The render loop targets 30 frames per second for normal motion and allows 60 while interacting; those are configured budgets, not measured physical-device guarantees. Hidden/offscreen views suspend rendering; paused/reduced-motion scenes render on demand outside active flight.

Labels provide 44-pixel targets, collision handling and reserved HUD space. The atlas and controls remain usable when WebGL is unavailable. Reduced motion removes orbital animation and camera tweening. Modals retain keyboard focus, bounded scrolling and translated accessible labels. Browser viewport tests do not establish physical iPhone/Android FPS, battery use or thermal behavior.
